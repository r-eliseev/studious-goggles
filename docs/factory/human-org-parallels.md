# The same problems in organizations of only humans

The owner's repeated observation (source 03: M16 "people have this too", M19, M05; source 05: W04) is that the questions behind a software factory are not new: they appear in any organization that delegates work. This table records each problem, its human-organization counterpart and solutions, what is different for agents, and where it appears in the owner's questions. Principle P1: kept even where the current harness does not use it. Labels as in the walkthrough: **✔** read on the page, **◐** search snippet or secondary, **[синтез]** our own reading.

| # | Problem | In organizations of humans | Source | What differs for agents | Owner's questions, catalog |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Delegating intent | mission command: centralized intent, decentralized execution, subordinates told the effect and the reason; trust "must be earned and sustained, includes tolerance for mistakes" (NATO AJP-01) ✔ [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_command) | read on page | executors lack accumulated context and cannot always ask back; trust has to be mechanized | W01; M12, M13; C60, C61 |
| 2 | Conflict of interest between the one who delegates and the one who acts | principal–agent problem ✔ [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal%E2%80%93agent_problem) (subtitle: "Conflict of interest when one person acts on another's behalf") | read on page | no personal incentives; "gaming" arises from optimization pressure, not self-interest | W01; M36; C30 |
| 3 | Right product versus product right | verification and validation (Boehm 1984): "Are we building the product right?" / "Are we building the right product?" ✔ [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_verification_and_validation) | read on page | spec and test are written by the same system, so both questions collapse unless verification is independent | W04; M08, M36; C30–C35 |
| 4 | Independent checking | two-person rule: critical actions require two or more authorized people ✔ [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-eyes_principle); independent QA and acceptance testing [синтез] | read on page | a second agent of the same model is not independent (Knight–Leveson on human programmers: independence rejected at 99%, ◐ [NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19870002808/downloads/19870002808.pdf); Kim et al. for LLMs ◐) | M32, M36; C34, C35 |
| 5 | Gaming a measure | Goodhart's law "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"; Campbell's law (1969) ✔ [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodhart%27s_law) | read on page | agents write `return true`, edit tests (StrongDM ✔, METR ◐) at machine speed | M14, M31, M36; C30, C31 |
| 6 | Tacit knowledge, "ask a colleague" | people ask; knowledge lives in heads, notes, ADRs (Nygard 2011 ◐) | [Cognitect](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions) ◐ | unattended agents cannot ask unless a deferred call exists (source 04); only what is written survives between runs | M15, M16, M17, M37; C25, C66, C81–C84 |
| 7 | Limited working memory | notes, handoffs, checklists, abstractions, search | [синтез] (M19) | context windows with compaction; subagents with clean context (Anthropic docs ✔) | M05, M19, M20; C80 |
| 8 | Conflicting sources of truth | hierarchy of trust, freshness, authority, "run it and see", ask the owner (M17) | [синтез] | agent needs explicit hierarchy and an obligation to report conflicts | M17; C82 |
| 9 | Understanding debt, bus factor | onboarding, explaining to others, rotating duties [синтез]; comprehension did not follow speed in a study (Qiao et al. ✔) | M25, M34 | faster output without growth of understanding; deliberate ordering of understanding needed | M25, M34; C64 |
| 10 | Learning from incidents | blameless postmortems (Google SRE book ◐); ADRs | [sre.google](https://sre.google/sre-book/postmortem-culture/) | each miss can become a new check automatically if the loop exists (skill loop, source 02) | M35, M26; C81, C100 |
| 11 | Anticipating failure | pre-mortem (Klein, HBR 2007 ◐) | M42 | agent can list failures before starting at almost no cost | M41–M43; C65 |
| 12 | Review as a bottleneck | review queues, reviewer fatigue | S02 W6; Shapiro ✔ "Your life is diffs" | volume rises by orders of magnitude, review must become risk-based and partly mechanical | W02; M39; C63, C40 |
| 13 | Accountability | the responsible person must explain the system | M25 | an agent has no stakes: the human owner answers, hence the owner's requirement to understand | M25, M34; C64 |
| 14 | Blast radius of mistakes | change management, backups, separation of duties, canary releases [синтез] | M40 | agents act faster and with the user's own credentials, so scoped tokens, protected state and rollback matter more | M40, M35; C45, C58 |
| 15 | Incentive to report | a quiet employee gets less attention; a noisy one is ignored | S04 A8 | outbox pacing and evals tune how much to say ("start quiet and earn attention") | W03; C24, C74 |
